Mentorship and Hackathon Contributions

UWB Hacks 2025 – Save the World! I had the honor of serving as a judge for UWB Hacks 2025: Save the World!, one of the largest and most vibrant student-led hackathons hosted by the University of Washington Bothell. This year’s event brought together over 500+ participants, with the support of 8 organizing student clubs, 40+ student officers, and 46 companies represented across judges, mentors, and volunteers—including professionals from Microsoft, Amazon, AWS, Meta, and Avanade.

Spanning a Month of Hacking, the event featured a wide range of preparatory activities including soldering workshops, DALL·E API demos, resume reviews, cybersecurity CTFs, and ideation sessions, culminating in a high-impact hackathon centered on the theme: "Save the World!". Participants were encouraged to create meaningful, socially impactful solutions across themed tracks, using any level of tech—from no-code to advanced stacks.

With 60+ first-round judges, 7 grand jury members, and 30+ mentors, the hackathon created a powerful platform for students to engage with industry professionals and showcase their ideas. I had the privilege of contributing to the evaluation process alongside an esteemed panel of experts, providing technical feedback and encouraging innovation.

HackIllinois 2025: As a judge for HackIllinois 2025, I assessed creative and impactful projects developed by talented participants. This experience reinforced my commitment to fostering innovation and supporting the next generation of problem solvers.

CougarHacks 2025: I served as a mentor for CougarHacks 2025, held from April 26th to April 27th, 2025. During this event, I provided guidance and support to participants, helping them refine their ideas and overcome technical challenges. It was a rewarding experience to contribute to the success of this hackathon and inspire innovation among the participants.

This hackathon exemplified how cross-disciplinary collaboration, community support, and student leadership can come together to drive real-world change.
