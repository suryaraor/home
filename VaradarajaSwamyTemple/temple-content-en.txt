### **A Vision in a Dream... Six Centuries of History... The Saga of Jagadevpur Temple in Siddipet!**

**The grand festival on Vaishakha Purnima... The fascinating story behind 1400 acres of temple land.**

**Siddipet:** In the Siddipet district of Telangana, in the Jagadevpur mandal, stands the Sri Varadaraja Swamy Temple in Vardarajpur. The village itself is named after the deity. There is also a Hanuman temple within the village limits. This temple, with a history spanning over six centuries, is surrounded by a captivating legend.

**Magnificent Brahmotsavam Celebrations**
Every year, during Vaishakha Purnima in May, the temple hosts a grand festival (Brahmotsavam). The celebrations are reminiscent of the Garuda Seva held in Vishnu Kanchi, Tamil Nadu. Devotees flock in large numbers to participate in these festivities.

**Historical Background of the Temple**
According to local lore, the temple was built in 1356 CE. The ancestors of the current temple chairman were transporting the idol of Varadaraja Swamy from Vishnu Kanchi (Kanchipuram, Tamil Nadu) to their native village, Rayagadh. On the way, they halted for the night at Jagadevpur. That night, one of them had a dream in which the deity appeared and instructed them to build a temple at that very spot. However, they felt the location was unsuitable and decided to install their family deity in their native village instead.

The next morning, as they resumed their journey, their cart broke down. It took the entire day to repair it. That night, the deity appeared again in a dream, reiterating the same instruction. When they shared this with the villagers and their followers, they refused to build the temple there. Once again, as they tried to leave, the same incident occurred. Interpreting this as the deity's will, they decided to construct the temple at that spot.

**The Nawab's Challenge... 1400 Acres of Land**
To obtain land for the temple, they approached the Nawab of Medak. The Nawab set a condition: he would grant as much land as the devotee could run with a pot of burning embers on his head. Driven by devotion, the devotee ran around 1400 acres with the pot on his head. Thus, the temple was built on the land acquired in this manner, with construction continuing for ten years.

**Current Status... Temple Features**
For the past six centuries, the descendants of those devotees have managed the temple. However, in recent times, due to lack of income, the Endowments Department has left the temple to the deity. The once vast 1400 acres have dwindled to just 70 acres due to encroachments over time.

In this temple, one can see golden lizards and silver monitor lizards. The temple walls bear inscriptions of its glorious past. The ancient well in the temple was recently closed. During the festival, the idol of Varadaraja Swamy is taken in a chariot procession to the Hanuman temple in the village. Devotees gather in large numbers to witness this grand event.
